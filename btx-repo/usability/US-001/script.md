# Facilitator Script — US-001: Consent Grant Flow

| Document | Script |
|---|---|
| Version | 1.0 |
| Date | 2026-05-17 |
| Facilitator | @ux-researcher |

> **Instructions to facilitator**: Words in regular text are said aloud. Words in [brackets] are instructions to you. Stay neutral — do not explain the UI or hint at answers. Use "What are you thinking?" or "Can you tell me more?" to encourage think-aloud. Do not say "correct" or "wrong."

---

## Welcome (0–5 min)

"Welcome, and thank you so much for participating today. My name is [facilitator name]. I'm on the BTX design team.

We are testing a new government portal that helps citizens control who can access their personal data. I want to be very clear: we are testing the website, not you. There are no right or wrong answers — we want to know where the design is confusing so we can fix it.

This session will take about 45 minutes. With your permission, I'd like to record the screen and your voice so I can review it later. The recording will only be seen by the design team and will be deleted after analysis. Your name will not be associated with anything we publish.

[Obtain verbal or written consent. Start recording.]

Do you have any questions before we start?"

---

## Warm-up (5–10 min)

1. "Can you tell me a little about how you use your smartphone day to day?"
2. "Have you ever shared your personal data — like your Aadhaar or bank details — with an app or website to get a service?"
3. "When you see a consent form or 'terms and conditions', what do you usually do?"

[Note responses. This is not scored — just context.]

---

## Task T-01 — Grant Consent (10–25 min)

[Share screen / open prototype. Navigate to W-01 (Notice screen).]

"I'm going to give you a scenario. Imagine you are applying for a home loan at a company called ABC Home Finance. As part of the process, a screen appears on your phone asking for your data consent. Please go ahead and complete whatever you need to do on this screen.

As you go, please think out loud — tell me what you're seeing, what you're thinking, what you expect to happen when you click something.

[Start timer.]

[Observe silently. Note:
  - Where the participant pauses
  - Any verbal confusion
  - Whether they read the data field list
  - Whether they read the "you can revoke" notice
  - Where they hesitate on W-03 checkbox]

[If stuck > 90 seconds without progress, prompt: "What would you normally do here?"]
[If stuck > 3 minutes, mark as abandoned and move on.]

[Stop timer when W-04 appears.]"

---

## Task T-02 — Revoke Consent (25–35 min)

"Now imagine you've thought about it and you want to take back the consent you just gave. Please show me how you'd do that."

[Observe. Note whether they:
  - Navigate to My Consents naturally
  - Find the Revoke button without help
  - Understand what happens after revoking]

---

## Post-task Questions (35–45 min)

**Comprehension (RQ-01)**  
"In your own words, what did you agree to just now? What data did you share, and with whom?"

[Do not prompt. Score: correct if they mention principal name AND at least one data field.]

**Revocability (RQ-02)**  
"Can you change your mind about that consent? If so, how?"

[Score: correct if they say they can revoke it and describe the mechanism (even vaguely).]

**Confusion / friction**  
"Was there anything on those screens that was confusing or unclear?"
"Was there any point where you weren't sure what to do next?"

**Trust**  
"How confident did you feel that your personal data would be kept safe? On a scale of 1 to 5, where 1 is not at all confident and 5 is very confident?"

**SUS Questionnaire**  
[Hand participant the 10-question SUS form or read questions aloud if phone session.]

---

## Debrief (45–50 min)

"That's everything we needed — thank you so much. 

The portal we tested is called Bharat Trust Exchange. It's designed to make sure you stay in control of your personal data when using government-linked digital services. Your feedback today will directly influence how we improve it.

Do you have any questions for me?"

[Answer questions. Do not discuss other participants' sessions. Stop recording. Note debrief observations.]
